Market volume

Make the player market carry real volume against free, suggested friend trades and instant entrustment, so it can later be put forward over friend trades.

What production showed

~50

cards sold a week on a six-day-old market, with 4 bids per sale on average

~3,000

cards entrusted a week. Supply goes to the Archives, because selling at the floor nets about the entrustment value

PiCoins idle in 20 wallets: money buys only market cards

Reframe: demand exists, and the market is short of sellers. It competes for supply with the Archives, not with friend trades, and only for the cards someone wants.

Locked decisions

  • Target: the cards someone wants. Bulk duplicates stay with the Archives.
  • Wanted: missing from another player's goals, or from a set they lack 1 or 2 cards of. Counted across all players, with one definition for buyers and sellers.
  • Seller table: the entrustment recap's "x players want it" opens one line per wanted card, prefilled: sell at the order's price, auction higher with a duration, or entrust anyway. One validation, and an "entrust them all" escape.
  • Buy orders: on a game across all editions, served only by editions whose minimum the price clears. Money is held, best price is served first, 7 days. A direct sale is instant and pays no stamp; listing higher notifies the order's owner.
  • Separation: orders never bid on their own. The order form shows open listings and buy-now; every listing form shows the best order.
  • Order entry points: missing cards in sets and goals, the market's empty search, and a suggestion on the goal.
  • Money matters: Archive resale joins this work, in the Brotherhood's shop and in market search results, priced well above players' offers as a ceiling; slots are bought in context at the limit; free up to 5 goals, 5 custom sets (a player holding 6 keeps them) and 3 decks; every slot beyond is bought with PiCoins.
  • Tab: not the lever. It goes back to the duels forge (option E'). Read the market's share of cards changing hands four weeks after orders ship.

Weak points that survived

  • Alt accounts can move money through direct sales for 10% commission; detection must cover them.
  • "N players want it" exposes goals in aggregate, accepted since only a count shows.
  • The auction stamp is still charged at listing, against the research's advice.
  • The "1 or 2 missing cards" share and the Legendaries nobody holds spare are untested.
  • No source measures any of this at about 30 players.
