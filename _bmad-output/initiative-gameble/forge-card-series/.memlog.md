- (crack) Tomes (a genre split into volumes of about 30 by release date) mean nothing to the player: they cannot recognize what they are completing
- (crack) The sets in the collection are unreadable: nobody wants to browse them to see what they are completing
- (direction) Sets must group things that make sense and that the player recognizes; the franchise is the model example
- (direction) Simplify and clarify sets and the collection, even if it means starting almost from scratch
- (note) Steam GetItems already returns franchises; the import does not store them today
- (lock) Every card always belongs to at least one series/set; no orphan card
- (decision) If there are many series, the HUD shows only the major ones; niche ones are found by searching
- (note) The import already asks GetItems for basic_info (developers, franchises) but keeps only the publisher, genres and tags
- (lock) Completion tiers (bronze/silver/gold, like Steam badges) keep large series motivating without splitting them into volumes
- (note) Candidate families: franchise, studio, theme (tags), era, hall of fame (Steam review stats), editorial (V3, by hand)
- (crack) Tiers lift the size limit: risk of falling back to giant, fuzzy series; recognition, not size, must stay the filter
- (lock) Series casting: Theme (backbone, covers the catalog, tiers); Franchise and Studio (small series you can finish, just "complete"); Hall of fame (prestige, tiers)
- (kill) Era (too vague) and Editorial (already V3) dropped; Publisher replaced by Studio, Tomes by Themes, Best rated of the year absorbed into Hall of fame
- (decision) A mix of sizes is wanted: large tiered series for the long run, small series you can finish for the satisfaction of "complete"
- (lock) A game joins every theme of the curated list that is among its 5 most-voted Steam tags (often 1 to 3 themes); one card can advance several series at once
- (lock) Safety net: a game with no theme in its top 5 tags joins the "UFOs" series (Les OVNI), owned as uncategorizable
- (assumption) With a list of about 30 well-chosen themes, the UFOs stay small (under 5% of the catalog); to check on real import data, and if it grows, fix the list, not the rule
- (lock) A "major" series depends on the player, not a fixed list: first "In progress" (their series sorted by closeness to the next tier or completion, plus those they just progressed in), then a short "To discover" row; everything else goes through search
- (crack) A fixed list of major series would recreate today's problem (30 rows, 25 empty); a purely personal list leaves a new player with an empty screen and hides series like the Hidden Gems
- (lock) The collection opens on series; a series opens as an album page: owned cards, empty slots for missing ones, tiers visible
- (decision) The full grid of every card, with filters, stays as a secondary view; it will serve trades and sales later
- (note) Today completing a set unlocks a card back (spec, card backs; epic-card-backs, post-MVP, consumes SetCompleted)
- (lock) Rewards: bronze/silver (theme, hall of fame) = celebration and a feed line; gold (theme, hall of fame) = a card back of that series; complete (franchise, studio, UFOs) = a "complete" stamp on the album page and a feed line, no back
- (crack) "Completing a set unlocks a back" would explode into hundreds of card backs with franchises and studios; the spec rule must become "gold on a large series unlocks a back"
- (direction) Customization rewards beyond card backs: menu theme, profile title
- (note) A card back is seen only by its player (spec); a profile title would be seen by others. The UI accent is already a CSS variable (--color-accent)
- (lock) Profile title (seen by everyone: profile, feed, trades): earned by completing a franchise or studio and at each tier of a theme or hall of fame (Apprentice/Adept/Master); generated from a template with no grammatical agreement ("Completionist · Dark Souls"); one active title
- (lock) Menu theme (accent colour, contrast checked): rare reward for a meta goal, N series at gold (e.g. 3, 10, 25)
- (crack) A title generated naively from the name gives ugly or badly agreed titles in French; the template must avoid any agreement
