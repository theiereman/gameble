Decks and the Top Trump duel

Outcome: hardened, then reworked on 2026-11-14 after a four-lens review found the first duel solved. Release 1.4 ships decks, weekly card stats and live duels between friends; ranked and deferred duels follow. Forged idea: forge-decks-and-matches.md.

Locked decisions

  • Live duels between two online friends first, to test the game; ranked duels and deferred duels come later with the same rounds.
  • A round: the caller locks a card and a stat with its direction; the card turns face up; the answerer, not knowing the call, picks a card from their hand; then all is revealed. Both sides read the other, so no move is always best.
  • Ten rounds, calls alternating from a random first caller; one hand of 3 serves both to call and to answer.
  • Both 12-card decks visible during the duel, played cards struck out; hands hidden; decks revealed only once both are locked. The last round is played with open hands.
  • Points, no capture; every stat frozen at the duel's start.
  • Timers of 30 s to call and 20 s to answer; the game plays on timeout; 60 s to reconnect; no win by forfeit.
  • Five stats played both ways: popularity, rating, release date, discovery, circulation (distinct holders, within its rarity). Rarity is out: it is the review count twice.
  • Stats computed at the deploy, then every Monday; decks evolve with what the Brotherhood does.
  • Decks of exactly 12 cards, a game at most once, up to 5 decks; a deck slot is an edition and blocks one copy.
  • First win of the UTC day gives a duel capsule, only if the game played none of the winner's moves; no stakes.
  • A private player cannot duel, as they cannot trade.

Rejected, and why

  • Asynchronous halves against an automatic defence: the best call was always the hand's most extreme stat, which the automatic play also made; an answerer in person had nothing to decide.
  • The call shown, the card hidden: the answer reduces to "my best on that stat or my worst".
  • Both hands visible: the caller finds a call nothing beats. Nothing visible: no reading, a flat endgame.
  • Automatic play that starts a half: rewards absent players and idle alts.
  • Capture until a deck is empty; 30-card decks; rarity quotas; copies owned or market price as a stat; circulation by copies; stakes; forfeit wins.
  • Only living stats, for now: 36 players cannot tell 12,434 cards apart.

Weak points that survived

  • Live play needs two friends online at once; with 29 weekly players, how often that happens is what 1.4 tests.
  • A newcomer's thin collection may lose most duels; to watch once real games exist.
  • A player can duel their own alt in two tabs for a daily capsule; accepted as no worse than the alt opening its own capsules.
  • A deck can weaken with no action from its owner; the weekly change arrows must make that legible.
  • The ranked queue will be thin at today's player count; its rules (unrated between friends, two meetings a week) are sketched, not tested.
