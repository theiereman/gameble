# D3 digest: faucet/sink economics and NPC buy-back/resale economies (r1-1)

Accessed 2026-11-24 for all sources. Budget: 15 tool calls, about 8 sources read or snippet-evaluated. "Snippet" means the claim comes from a search-result summary, not a full page read, so its confidence is lowered.

## Findings

1. **Claim:** OSRS added its Grand Exchange tax on 9 Dec 2021: 1% on sales, rounded down, no tax under 100 gp, capped at 5m gp per item, starter tools and bonds exempt. Part of the tax goes into a "virtual coffer" that buys 20 listed high-end items from sellers, up to a daily quota, and deletes them. When the coffer is empty or the quota is used up, the sale goes to players as normal. The page does not say what share of the tax goes to the coffer. Jagex said it would "start small" so it could watch how much money enters and leaves the game.
   - Source: https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Update:Grand_Exchange_Tax_%26_Item_Sink
   - Publisher: OSRS Wiki (mirror of a Jagex news post) · pub_date 2021-12 · confidence high · class mechanism

2. **Claim:** The tax rate rose from 1% to 2% on 29 May 2025.
   - Source: https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Talk:Grand_Exchange (snippet)
   - Publisher: OSRS Wiki talk page · pub_date n.d. · confidence medium · class number

3. **Claim:** A causal-inference study of the OSRS interventions found that the transaction tax "did not meaningfully affect the trading volume of items at the tax boundaries." It also found the item sink "contributed to the inflation of luxury good prices, without reducing trade volume," and the illicit gold market was "relatively unaffected." The abstract gives no figures; I did not read the full paper.
   - Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07970
   - Publisher: arXiv (Hogan-Hennessy, Xenopoulos, Silva) · pub_date 2022-10 · confidence high for the abstract claims · class outcome

4. **Claim:** Coverage of the OSRS tax says it exists to fight inflation by removing gp from circulation. It also says most of the tax is deleted and a small part pays for the item buy-and-delete. One source attributes a "62% of gold leaving" figure to death costs, but that source is weak.
   - Source: https://www.gameslearningsociety.org/wiki/why-did-runescape-add-a-tax/ (snippet)
   - Publisher: Games Learning Society wiki · pub_date n.d. · confidence low (the snippet contradicts itself on the rate) · class mechanism

5. **Claim:** Player sentiment: a later tax increase drew loud protest, a staged "Boston Tea Party" by players.
   - Source: https://www.pcgamer.com/games/mmo/american-old-school-runescape-players-are-recreating-the-boston-tea-party-after-jagex-hiked-taxes-they-took-a-page-from-the-british-empire-rather-poetic-from-a-british-game
   - Publisher: PC Gamer · pub_date 2025 (approximate, from headline context) · confidence medium · class sentiment

6. **Claim:** CCP measures the EVE economy with four price indices (Chained Laspeyres CPI, Mineral, Primary Producer and Secondary Producer; base 100), ISK faucets and sinks by category, and "ISK velocity". Velocity is the 30-day moving sum of market trade divided by active ISK, and it was "just under 0.5" and falling in 2015. CCP read the decline as players "sitting on more isk." Active ISK had grown steadily since launch. The page sets no inflation or money-supply growth target and announces no sink intervention.
   - Source: https://www.eveonline.com/news/view/eve-economy-update-eve-vegas-2015-report
   - Publisher: CCP (CCP Quant) · pub_date 2015-11 · confidence high · class mechanism

7. **Claim:** The EVE Monthly Economic Report publishes a sinks-and-faucets chart broken down by category, a "Top 6 Commodity Faucets" chart, and a CSV covering the last 3 years. In April 2016 sinks exceeded faucets, which forum readers called a first for that data. CCP has published errata, for example a mismatch between the sinks/faucets data and the money-supply data.
   - Source: https://forums.eveonline.com/t/monthly-economic-report-february-2021/302188 (snippet), https://www.eveonline.com/de/news/view/monthly-economic-report-october-2025 (snippet)
   - Publisher: CCP / EVE forums · pub_date 2021-02, 2025-10 · confidence medium · class mechanism

8. **Claim:** In the August 2026 MER thread, players cite the "Oz Report" warning of "too much isk in the system" and ask CCP for more sinks. The search found no CCP reply.
   - Source: https://forums.eveonline.com/t/monthly-economic-report-august-2026/517992 (snippet)
   - Publisher: EVE forums · pub_date 2026-09 · confidence low · class sentiment

9. **Claim:** Albion Online's Black Market is an NPC in Caerleon that only places buy orders for player-crafted gear. What it buys becomes the loot that PvE mobs and chests drop, so the drops depend on what players sell it. Players cannot buy from it. Fan guides say a share of items is "discarded", which acts as an item sink, and that it buys only the exact item and quality. This is the closest live example of an NPC that recycles player items back into the game. It feeds the items back as loot, not through a shop, so there is no buy-from-NPC price to arbitrage.
   - Source: https://www.albiondatabase.com/guides/black-market (snippet), https://gamingonlinux.com/2017/06/albion-onlines-new-black-market-feature-is-sounding-really-interesting (snippet)
   - Publisher: fan guide; GamingOnLinux · pub_date n.d.; 2017-06 · confidence medium · class mechanism

10. **Claim:** In Hearthstone, disenchanting returns a fixed fraction of a card's crafting cost: common 40 to craft and 5 back (1/8), rare 100 and 20 (1/5), epic 400 and 100 (1/4), legendary 1600 and 400 (1/4). For about two weeks after a card is nerfed, it disenchants for its full crafting cost. The wide buy/sell spread is what keeps destroy-then-craft from being a loop; the temporary full refund is the "fairness" valve when Blizzard devalues a card.
    - Source: https://support.blizzard.com/en/article/20456 (snippet), https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Disenchanting (snippet)
    - Publisher: Blizzard support; Hearthstone wiki · pub_date n.d. · confidence medium-high · class number

11. **Claim:** NPC vendors with infinite stock and infinite cash set a fixed ceiling price (their sell price) and a fixed floor price (their buy price) for items. Junk items in a saturated market drift down to the vendor floor. The author argues that item faucets matter more than cash faucets: "Worry more about equipment faucets and drains than cash faucets and drains."
    - Source: https://gamedeveloper.com/design/the-f-words-of-mmos-faucets
    - Publisher: Game Developer (Simon Ludgate) · pub_date 2011-07 · confidence high that the article says this · class theory

12. **Claim:** NPC arbitrage loops appear when vendor buy and sell prices drift apart: Ultima Online after vendor price resets (buy at 188, sell to another NPC at 199), and a Guild Wars 2 vendor mispricing. The usual fix is a wide spread between vendor buy and sell prices. One Ragnarok private server pays out only 1/8 of an item's buy price when a player sells it to an NPC.
    - Source: https://community.stratics.com/threads/jeremy-npc-vendors-price-bug.57552/ (snippet; forum), https://the-ragnarok-classic-1.gitbook.io/wiki/custom-implementation/sell-pricing (snippet), https://games.slashdot.org/story/12/08/31/1831231/arenanet-suspends-digital-sales-of-guild-wars-2 (snippet)
    - Publisher: Stratics forum; private-server wiki; Slashdot · pub_date n.d.; n.d.; 2012-08 · confidence medium · class mechanism

13. **Claim:** Published rules of thumb for the sink/faucet ratio disagree:
    - faucets at about 1.1 to 1.3 times sinks per progression tier;
    - sinks at 90 to 110% of daily faucet income;
    - sink removal within ±20% of midgame net income.

    All three come from vendor or consultancy blogs, not from developers of a live game. Unity advises reading the ratio together with inflation, velocity and days-of-currency-on-hand.
    - Source: https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/game-economy-balancing-spreadsheets, https://pulsegeek.com/articles/tune-repair-costs-and-durability-sinks-step-by-step/, https://unity.com/resources/game-economy-design-guide (all snippets)
    - Publisher: StraySpark; PulseGeek; Unity · pub_date n.d. · confidence low (unsourced heuristics) · class number

14. **Claim:** Lehdonvirta and Castronova's *Virtual Economies: Design and Analysis* (MIT Press, 2014) defines faucets as what adds new goods or money and sinks as what removes them. A later chapter covers running a live economy. I did not read the text.
    - Source: https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt9qf5t6 (snippet)
    - Publisher: MIT Press / JSTOR · pub_date 2014 · confidence medium · class theory

## Leads worth chasing

- Full text of arXiv 2210.07970: effect sizes for the item sink's luxury-price inflation. This is the key evidence for "an NPC that buys and deletes can raise prices".
- The OSRS 2025 tax increase to 2% and Jagex's stated reasons (Jagex news post). Check whether Jagex published how much gp the tax removes.
- EVE MER CSV "sinks and faucets over time": compute real sink/faucet ratios over years. Did CCP ever state a target?
- Lehdonvirta & Castronova, chapter on managing live economies, for any money-supply growth rule.
- The Guild Wars 2 Trading Post fee (5% listing plus 10% on sale) as a two-part auction sink, from an official source.
- Albion official patch notes on the Black Market's discard rate and how it sets prices.
- Gurto, Ryan & Blair, FDG 2011 (https://cgi.cse.unsw.edu.au/~blair/pubs/2011GurtoRyanBlairFDG.pdf). The PDF would not parse as text; it may cover NPC pricing.

## Looked for, not found

- Any developer-stated target ratio of sinks to faucets, or a target growth rate for the money supply (searched CCP and Jagex). None found; only vendor-blog heuristics (finding 13).
- A measured figure for how much gold the OSRS tax removes.
- Any game that sizes its sink budget as a percentage of last period's faucet, the analogue of "50% of last week's minted currency". Not found.
- An authoritative postmortem of a buy-from-NPC, resell-to-players exploit in a game whose NPC resells items players destroyed. Only forum threads and vendor-mispricing cases turned up.
- Evidence from small communities (under a few hundred players) on sink sizing. Only one private-server pricing page turned up.
- WoW gold sinks and WoW Token, Hypixel, Diablo, and a GDC Vault talk: not reached within budget.
