# D2 — Pricing and allocation mechanisms (round 1, lane 1)

Accessed 2026-11-24 for every source. Budget: 15 tool calls, 8 sources read (some through search snippets only, flagged below).

## Findings

### Dutch / descending-price auctions

1. **Claim:** A descending pay-as-bid (Dutch) sale creates "a big game of chicken": each bidder has a reason to wait if they think others will not bid, which lowers the clearing price. When everyone waits, bids bunch up at once, and bidders also rush in near the end at what looks like the lowest price.
   - Source: https://a16zfrontier.com/nft-sales-market-clearing-gas-wars-auction-mechanism-design-for-builders/ — a16z crypto (Kominers & Roughgarden)
   - pub_date 2022-09 · confidence high · class mechanism
2. **Claim:** Under pay-as-bid Dutch, top fans who buy early at high prices may pay far more than later buyers (fairness problem). The authors' fix is uniform-price settlement: the price still descends, but every winner pays the final clearing price, so bidding early carries no penalty. That removes most of the reason to wait, and they call the result economically efficient.
   - Source: same a16z article · pub_date 2022-09 · confidence high · class mechanism/theory
3. **Claim:** Selling below the market-clearing price causes access races (gas wars, "jumping the line"). Optimising the contract does not fix this, because the race comes from demand above supply at that price.
   - Source: same a16z article · pub_date 2022-09 · confidence high · class mechanism
4. **Claim:** The a16z article gives no guidance on the starting price, the decay rate or the size of price steps.
   - Source: same a16z article · pub_date 2022-09 · confidence high · class mechanism (negative)
5. **Claim:** Art Blocks offered linear Dutch auctions (the price drops at fixed intervals) and exponential ones (the artist sets a half-life). Some releases ran a "Dutch auction with settlement": the lowest winning price sets the clearing price and earlier buyers can claim the difference back. Dutch auctions were brought in during the 2021 frenzy to slow down instant sell-outs.
   - Source: https://help.artblocks.io/Project-Pricing-Dutch-Auction-Settings-46eb5e031db549c6ad1324b5bab0387b (page did not render: Notion) plus search snippets of https://www.theblock.co/post/118888/art-blocks-hit-block-reorg-frenzied-nft-auction — Art Blocks / The Block
   - pub_date n.d. / 2021-09 · confidence medium (snippet-level) · class mechanism
6. **Claim:** Yuga Labs cancelled its planned Dutch auction for the Otherside (Otherdeed) mint and switched to a fixed price. The fixed-price mint then produced a large gas war (reported at over $150M of gas burned), and Yuga said it would refund gas spent on failed transactions.
   - Source: https://coingape.com/just-in-yuga-labs-cancels-otherside-auction-apecoin-rallies-past-26/ ; https://decrypt.co/99256/bored-ape-creators-slammed-nightmare-ethereum-nft-la — crypto press, search snippets only
   - pub_date 2022-04/05 · confidence medium · class outcome
7. **Claim:** Illuvium ran its land sale as a Dutch auction on a layer 2 and kept the average fee near $20 per transaction. Cited as an example of a Dutch auction spreading demand over time.
   - Source: https://cointelegraph.com/news/72m-illuvium-nft-land-dutch-auction-saves-buyers-thousands-on-gas-fees — Cointelegraph, snippet only
   - pub_date 2022-06 (approx.) · confidence low · class outcome
8. **Claim:** Revenue in a Dutch auction depends on how fast the clock moves. In lab experiments, a fast clock brought in significantly less than a sealed-bid auction, while a slow enough clock brought in more. The authors explain this with bidder impatience: waiting costs something, so a slow clock pushes people to buy earlier and higher. Standard theory, which treats Dutch and first-price sealed-bid auctions as equivalent, misses this, so clock speed is a design parameter. The authors also suggest that clock speed may explain the lab-versus-field gap (Cox et al. 1982 vs Lucking-Reiley 1999).
   - Source: https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/expeco/v11y2008i4p344-357.html (Katok & Kwasnica, *Experimental Economics* 11(4):344-357), via the abstract and search summary; full text not read
   - pub_date 2008 · confidence medium-high · class theory
   - Note: the lab clocks ran in seconds. A one-step-per-night clock is far slower than anything tested, so carrying the result over is an inference, not evidence.

### Market-pegged pricing and price-index protection

9. **Claim:** The Grand Exchange guide price in Old School RuneScape updates from trade history. Jagex does not publish the algorithm. Low-volume items (party hats, for example) were said to update only every few days or weekly "and can be inaccurate". A 30 September 2026 update moved guide prices to every 3 hours (from 24), with a cap of 20% change per update (from 5%), and low-volume items now update more often. Jagex can also set prices itself when "price manipulation is occurring on an inexcusable scale".
   - Source: https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Grand_Exchange — OSRS Wiki
   - pub_date n.d. (page carries an outdated-info banner) · confidence medium-high · class mechanism/number
10. **Claim:** WoW's TradeSkillMaster market value (DBMarket) is built to resist manipulation:
    - it keeps at most the lowest 30% of listings;
    - past the first 15%, a jump of 20% or more between consecutive listings cuts that listing and everything above it;
    - it then drops points more than 1.5 standard deviations from the mean and averages the rest;
    - daily values are blended over 14 days with a recency half-life of "a little over 2 days".

    The design guards against high-volume listings at inflated prices. Note that it is built on listings, not completed sales.
    - Source: https://support.tradeskillmaster.com/tsm-addon-documentation/auctiondb-market-value — TradeSkillMaster docs
    - pub_date n.d. · confidence high · class mechanism/number
11. **Claim:** In Path of Exile, players report manipulation through fake listings. Items are listed far below or above value with no intent to sell, which skews the prices others copy and the aggregators (poe.ninja) read. poe.ninja exposes a `listingCount` behind each price, which signals how thin the data is.
    - Source: https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/3675771 ; https://poe.ninja/docs/api — player forum / poe.ninja docs, snippet only
    - pub_date n.d. · confidence low · class sentiment/mechanism

### Per-player limits, global stock, sinks

12. **Claim:** Grand Exchange buy limits cap how many of an item an account can buy every 4 hours. The timer runs from the first purchase and does not roll, and related items share one limit. Wikis attribute the limits to preventing price manipulation and bulk-buy-then-resell merchanting; the wiki text does not quote Jagex stating the purpose. There is no sell limit.
    - Source: https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Grand_Exchange/Buying_limits ; https://runescape.wiki/w/Trading_and_merchanting_guide (snippet) — OSRS / RS Wiki
    - pub_date n.d. · confidence high for the mechanism, medium for the purpose · class mechanism
13. **Claim:** The Old School RuneScape item sink is a direct analogue of an NPC buying from the player market. Jagex spends part of the Grand Exchange tax (1% since 2021-12, 2% since 2025-05, capped at 5M per item) to buy a fixed number of chosen high-value items from players each week and delete them. Mod Ash estimated about 4.1 trillion coins spent on sink purchases as of 2025-05.
    - Source: https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Grand_Exchange — OSRS Wiki
    - pub_date n.d. · confidence high · class mechanism/number
14. **Claim:** A causal study of Old School RuneScape found that the item sink contributed to price inflation of luxury goods without reducing trade volume. The transaction tax did not meaningfully change trade volume at the tax boundaries.
    - Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.07970 — Hogan-Hennessy, Xenopoulos, Silva (arXiv)
    - pub_date 2022-10 · confidence medium (abstract only, no effect sizes read) · class outcome
