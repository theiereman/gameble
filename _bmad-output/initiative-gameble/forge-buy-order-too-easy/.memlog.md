- (decision) Subject is C: the buy order and the instant paths together, a buyer gets a card at a set price without any auction
- (note) Code check: player listings have no buy-now; the order form links open listings to their auction; the only instant buy is the Archives offer (fixed price). The buyer's order is served when a seller presses sell now
- (crack) Rich players can buy everything: PiCoins alone fill any gap, through the uncapped Archive shop and high orders that always come first
- (crack) Player interaction becomes incidental: an order is an impersonal request that any player entrusting a copy fills, nearly automatically
- (note) Checked: Confier sets wanted copies apart and the table prefills sell-to-order when the order is at 90% of the average price or more; one press validates; the seller never learns who ordered, the buyer sees the seller's name only after
- (decision) The interaction to protect is a trade that needs talk (negotiation), not trade volume
- (direction) Also limit automatic trades (4 duplicates for 4 duplicates); this reaches past buy orders to friend trades and the trade suggestions
- (note) Evidence: 56% of trade sides sit at the 4-card limit, so many of the 1,100 friend trades a week may already be bulk duplicate swaps; an epic for trade suggestions exists
- (decision) A card deserves talk by its rarity (above a threshold to set) and by what it means to the buyer (last card of a set, a tier unlocked)
- (crack) Contradicts the shipped design: market volume was built to route exactly the cards missing from goals (wanted = missing from goals or sets 1-2 cards short) through orders, and goals suggest ordering their missing cards
- (crack) Trades are only between friends; with about 30 players a missing rare card may have no friend holder, so blocking instant paths can leave a player with no path but auctions or luck
- (direction) Direct sale to an order only for Common and Uncommon; Rare and above go through auction or trade
- (crack) Commons and Uncommons come from capsules in minutes (spec target), so orders limited to them buy almost nothing a player cannot pull; the order loses most of its value
- (crack) The Archive shop sells Rare at 500 and Epic at 1500 instantly and uncapped; a rarity rule for direct sales leaves the richest instant path open unless the shop follows it, yet the shop is the main PiCoin sink (destroyed/created ratio at 8%)
- (decision) Split by rarity: Common and Uncommon sell only to orders (or are entrusted), never at auction; Rare and above sell only at auction, never to an order in one press
- (crack) An order targets a game across its editions; with no auction for Common and Uncommon, a collector after one specific season's edition of a Common has no way to target it, and high-priced Common editions (a notable sale starts at 150) lose their only path
- (note) Seller maths for a Rare at auction at its minimum: 100 less 10 commission less 40 stamp = 50, the entrustment value; listing pays only above the floor, as today
- (decision) Rare and above: the order stays as a signal, its price held and shown to sellers, but a sale goes through an auction
- (crack) Money held twice: the order holds its price and a bid on the auction holds its maximum; an order that bids by itself was rejected (money held twice or moved silently)
- (lock) Rare and above: a seller can open an auction backed by the best order; the order becomes the first bid at its price, its held money moves to the bid (never held twice), its owner is told; an order never bids by itself on an auction it was not opened against
- (note) The November rejection of 'an order sale always opens an auction' (seller loses the instant sale) no longer holds: the seller gets a guaranteed floor at the order's price, delayed by the auction's duration
- (crack) Several orders on one game: only the best becomes the first bid; the others' owners must bid by hand and hold their money twice, or their orders also turn into bids, which is automatic bidding
- (crack) A seller's alt can place a high order and back an auction with it, so rivals must bid above a fake floor; the alt signals cover direct sales to a tied buyer, not order-backed auctions
- (lock) When an auction is opened against an order, every order on that game reaching the copy's rarity becomes a bid with its price as its maximum; the auction prices them as usual (second maximum plus an increment); no money is held twice
- (assumption) An order beaten in an order-backed auction ends and its money returns, as an outbid maximum does; it does not go back to being an open order
- (note) Accepted trade-off: this is automatic bidding, limited to auctions opened against orders, chosen over double holding and last-minute sniping by a second order
- (lock) Archive shop above Uncommon: a per-player cap per period (for example 1 Rare a day, 1 Epic a week), a back-office setting; Common and Uncommon stay uncapped; the shop stays the PiCoin sink without letting money buy everything
- (lock) Common and Uncommon: an order can target one precise edition as well as the game across editions; collectors compete by order price, best first, since these rarities have no auction
- (assumption) Still one open order per player and game, targeting either any edition or one edition
- (kill) The 'last card of a set' criterion: the rarity split already covers it, and blocking that card's order would hurt the player who needs it most
- (direction) Automatic friend trades (4 duplicates for 4) and trade suggestions: forge separately
