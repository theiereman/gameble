gameble — a Steam catalog TCG

A web TCG where every card is a Steam game: free boosters, prestige-based rarity, an auction market and a mock-solemn Gamblers' Brotherhood. Fully free-to-play.

Outcome

Both founding assumptions held up after the needed changes. The Steam API exposes enough data to rank games. The idea becomes a real game once cards come from boosters drawn from the whole catalog rather than from a player's own library.

Locked decisions

  • Card source: the whole Steam catalog via boosters, not the player's library.
  • Catalog: games only (no DLC, soundtracks, demos or tools) with ≥ 500 reviews, grouped into sets by genre, decade and publisher.
  • Rarity = prestige: review score, Metacritic and review volume. A player can guess it in one sentence.
  • Stability: each copy keeps its minted rarity and season forever, and the catalog and rarities are recomputed each season.
  • Loop: 1 free booster per 10 min. Destroy cards for a little currency, then bid at auction for rarer ones.
  • No real money anywhere.
  • Sinks: Gambler's Tax Stamp (listing fee) and Brotherhood Effort (~10% sale commission), destroyed and shown upfront.
  • Season goal: a parody Brotherhood Pass. Tiers are advanced by everyone's fees and reward contributors with rarer boosters, never currency.
  • Tone: The Grand Gamblers' Brotherhood, with mock-patriotic copy. Bilingual FR/EN UI.
  • Art: official Steam images from Steam's CDN, with publisher and copyright credited and a non-affiliation notice in the site terms.
  • Accounts: Steam OpenID login only. Market access is gated by gameble seniority.

Rejected, and why

  • Library-as-collection: nothing to obtain, so rarity is meaningless.
  • Obscurity-based rarity: famous games common, shovelware legendary.
  • Live rarity recomputation: destabilizes the market and owned value.
  • Any real money: loot-box/gambling regulatory exposure.
  • Email/password or generic OAuth login: trivially farmable.
  • Generated-only card art: less desirable to collect.
  • "Players are shareholders" theme: too heavy for a light joke.
  • Battle mode: not wanted.

Deferred

  • Exotic editions (delisted games, old classics), likely hand-curated.
  • Personal library layer: achievement and playtime badges on owned cards.
  • Daily bonus, premium boosters, crafting.

Weak points that survived

  • No fallback if a publisher asks to take down its art.
  • Steam API rate limits are unmeasured for a seasonal recompute of 10k–20k games.
  • Bot farming is still possible for collection and prestige; only the market is gated.
  • The market-access threshold is not chosen yet.
