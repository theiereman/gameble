- (assumption) Available Steam stats (playtime, achievements, global achievement %, reviews, current players, price, Metacritic) are enough to derive a rarity — to verify
- (crack) If rarity = f(game stats) and cards = owned games, rarity is not TCG rarity (nothing to obtain in-app) — central question: where do cards come from?
- (decision) Cards come from the whole Steam catalog via boosters (option B); not derived from the player's library
- (kill) Option A (library = collection) rejected: nothing to obtain in-app, rarity meaningless
- (direction) Later layer: link unlocked cards to the player's personal Steam library to add badges (achievements unlocked, etc.)
- (note) Consequence: Steam account/public profile becomes optional — core loop works without it
- (note) Open branch: catalog scope — Steam has 100k+ apps incl. shovelware/DLC/soundtracks; which apps become cards?
- (decision) Card usage: collect + trade between players + auction market
- (decision) Booster opening rate limit: 1 per 10 minutes per player
- (kill) Battle mode not in scope (not requested)
- (crack) 1 booster/10 min = up to 144/day for a scripted or always-on player; with a tradable market this inflates supply and enables multi-account farming
- (crack) Auction market requires a currency: its source, sinks and link to real money are undefined; real money + random boosters + tradable value = loot-box/gambling regulatory exposure
- (lock) Fully free-to-play, no real money anywhere (no purchase, no cash-out) — for now
- (kill) Paid currency/boosters and real-money cash-out rejected for now (loot-box/gambling exposure)
- (note) No real money lowers bot-farming incentive but does not remove it (leaderboard/prestige still motivates); in-game currency source and sinks still to define
- (crack) Rarity semantics ambiguous: 'rare' could mean obscure (few reviews/players) or prestigious (high rating/acclaim); pure popularity-based rarity makes famous games common and shovelware legendary
- (lock) Rarity = prestige (acclaimed/cult games are legendary), based on review score + Metacritic + review volume; must be guessable by a player in one sentence
- (lock) Special 'exotic' editions outside the normal scale for notable cases (e.g. games delisted from Steam, 20+ year classics)
- (kill) Obscurity-based rarity (few players = rare) rejected: counter-intuitive
- (crack) Delisted games: Steam store API often returns no details for delisted apps; detecting/curating them may need manual or third-party data
- (lock) Catalog = Steam apps of type game only (no DLC/soundtrack/demo/tool) with >= 500 Steam reviews; estimated 10k-20k cards, to confirm with real data
- (lock) Collection structured into sets (genre, year/decade, publisher); a game can belong to several sets
- (decision) Exotic editions deferred (out of scope for now) — supersedes earlier exotic lock
- (crack) Steam stats drift over time (reviews, review bombing, new hits): a card's computed rarity could change after it is owned/traded
- (lock) Each card copy keeps the rarity and season it was minted with, forever (later stat changes never alter owned cards)
- (lock) Seasons: catalog and rarities are recomputed once per season (e.g. quarterly); new games enter only at season start; cards carry their season (1st-edition effect)
- (kill) Live rarity recomputation rejected: destabilizes market and owned value
- (lock) Single in-game currency earned only by selling/destroying cards to the system for a tiny fixed amount (by rarity); accumulated currency is spent bidding on rarer cards at auction
- (crack) With destroy-to-system as the only faucet and no sink, total currency only grows (auctions just move it between players): long-term price inflation penalizes newcomers
- (lock) Auction sale tax (~10% of final price, destroyed) is the only currency sink; daily bonus, premium boosters and crafting left out for now

- (lock) Auction fees are shown transparently before listing and feed a collective season goal; when reached, every contributor receives a rarer booster (rewards paid in boosters, never credits, so the sink holds)
