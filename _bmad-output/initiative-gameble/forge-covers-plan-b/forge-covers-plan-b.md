# Plan B for Steam cover art in the store apps

**Plan:** publish the store apps with every cover. If an owner objects, withdraw the art fast and talk to them. No covers-free variant.

- **Scope:** store apps are the exposed surface; a withdrawal hides the game's art everywhere, website included.
- **Why not "no art":** the real image is the value of a card; a replacement changes the game.
- **Takedown:** a public form (one game, a list of games, or a publisher name) creates a request and mails the author a signed one-click hide link. The author validates, then the art is replaced by a placeholder. Commitment: 24 h; if the author has neither validated nor refused by then, the art is hidden automatically.
- **Store review refusal over IP:** appeal with the evidence (Valve's answer, the takedown process). Never ship hidden or removed covers to get through review. If the appeal fails, do not publish on that store. Google first (25 USD once, 14-day closed test), Apple after (99 USD a year).
- **Accepted risk:** share previews already published are not chased; a stored preview can keep showing retired art.
- **Rejected:** hiding covers during review then restoring them (store-policy violation, risks the developer account); automatic hide on every submission (replaced by the timer); a covers-free store build.
- **Not settled:** Valve's reply; the placeholder wording (a neutral message may serve the discussion better than a joke); the legal standing of this approach, which was not reviewed by a lawyer.
