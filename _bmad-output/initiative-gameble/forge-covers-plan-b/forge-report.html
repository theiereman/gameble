Plan B for Steam cover art in the store apps

Outcome: hardened. The covers stay; the plan B is a fast, validated withdrawal, not a covers-free build.

Locked decisions

  • The store apps are the exposed surface; a withdrawal hides the game's art everywhere, website included.
  • The real image is the value of a card; a replacement art changes the game, so there is no covers-free variant.
  • A public form takes one game, a list, or a publisher name and mails the author a signed one-click hide link.
  • Commitment of 24 hours; if the author has neither validated nor refused by then, the art is hidden automatically.
  • If a store refuses the app over IP, appeal with the evidence; if the appeal fails, do not publish on that store. Google first, Apple after.

Rejected and why

  • Hiding covers during review and restoring them: violates store policy and risks the developer account.
  • Automatic hide on every submission: replaced by the 24-hour timer to limit abuse while never leaving art up unattended.
  • A store build without covers: it breaks the game.

Weak points that survived

  • Withdrawing on request does not give a legal safe harbour for art the site copies itself (not reviewed by a lawyer).
  • An owner can complain directly to Apple, Google or Valve without using the form.
  • Stored share previews already published keep showing retired art; the author chose not to chase them.
  • Valve's reply is pending; the placeholder wording is still open.
