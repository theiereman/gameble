Seasons carry the collection over

Publishing a season left players with an empty collection, sets back to zero and the total counter at 0. The idea came out hardened: seasons become reprints, and nothing earned is ever wiped.

Locked decisions

  • Season = reprint. Sets are the same across seasons; any edition of a game counts for them. A new season recomputes rarities and adds games; progress carries on.
  • Editions. A copy keeps its minted rarity and season. Destruction value follows the minted rarity (no arbitrage); market value is left to players, who see the edition.
  • Earned stays earned. Tiers and rewards are kept forever with their season's mark. A lost tier is earned again with the new season's mark, which is what veterans chase.
  • Live percentage. Recomputed on the current set; a drop reads as new cards to find, never a loss.
  • Seasonal allocation screen. Rocket League style, at the first visit after a publication: what you unlocked last season and what follows you. One Brotherhood line heads it.
  • Counter and grid. The total counts distinct games across editions and never drops. One tile per game, best rarity owned, an edition count.
  • No clutter at 30 seasons. No season chips in the grid; a set shows its latest mark and a count (“Gold · 12 seasons”), detail on demand.

Rejected

  • Season = expansion. Sets are themes and franchises, not dated expansions; several “Roguelike” sets side by side would confuse.
  • Showing the highest tier ever reached. Replaced by the live tier plus past season marks.
  • A chip season selector in the collection. Loses its purpose and saturates; at most an edition filter.

Weak points that survived

  • Migration cost is unknown. Set and tier rows are per season today; whether production has real players decides how heavy the change is.
  • Season marks may cost art. A watermark over the existing back is cheap; a new style per season is not.
  • The Brotherhood rule was loosened. “At most one line per screen, at collective moments” must hold, or the satire spreads everywhere.
  • First discoveries and V2 prices are still counted per card-season; whether they move to per game is open.
