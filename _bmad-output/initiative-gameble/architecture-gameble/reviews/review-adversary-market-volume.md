# Adversarial review: market volume additions to the spine

Date: 2026-11-20. Scope: AD-58 to AD-63 and the 2026-11-20 amendments (AD-10, AD-35, AD-40 to AD-48, AD-52), read with AD-3, AD-6, AD-9, AD-10, AD-13 and AD-41 to AD-48. Decisions: `.memlog.md` from "2026-11-20: update run for market volume". Code read: `Game::Market::AuctionSettlement`, `Game::MoveHoldings`, `Game::Market::SumArchiveStocks`, `Game::Market::Notice`, `Game::Market::Handlers::WantedNotices`, `Game::Market::WantingPlayers`, `Game::GoalNeeds`, `Game::AddGoal`, `Game::GoalSteps`, `Game::BankLock`, `Accounts::UnbanPlayer`.

Method: for each hole, two stories built by different people, each obeying every AD to the letter, that still build incompatibly. Each hole ends with the rule that closes it. The spine was not edited.

## Verdict

Not ready to slice. The money core holds: every order status change runs under the owner's bank lock and changes status only from `open`, the order index on the ledger refuses a second hold, release or serving, the Archive and slot purchases are destroyed and bounded by the no-loss floor, and the stock job and `BuyFromArchives` serialise on the stock row. But one critical hole remains. AD-59 tells the direct sale to reuse a settlement step that releases a reservation, and that corrupts `reserved`. Four high holes leave the wanted table, the commission and the notices to the reader's interpretation. Most of the medium holes come from one source: `ApplyWantedChoices` composes steps that were each written to run alone in their own transaction.

## Holes

- **[critical]** *Story A (direct sale, AD-59)* does what AD-59 says: "it moves the copy as AD-45's settlement step does", and the Costs line has "the settlement step serves both a listing and an order". The step in code today (`AuctionSettlement#settle_sale`) moves the seller's copy with `released: { card => 1 }`, because a listing reserved that copy. *Story B (decks, trades and listings, AD-52, AD-8, AD-44)* holds a deck block, a trade offer or an open listing on the same edition in `reserved`. A direct sale sells a *free* copy, which nothing reserved, but the reused step still decrements `reserved`. Take quantity 2 and reserved 1: after the sale it is 1 and 0, and AD-6's check passes. The deck block is gone, so the deck card can be traded, listed or entrusted (exactly what AD-52 prevents). If the reservation belonged to an open listing, its later settlement subtracts 1 from 0, the check fails, `CloseAuction` raises on every run and the leader's maximum stays committed for good. `BuyFromArchives` ("as AD-45's settlement step does for a winner") is safe only because the winner side releases nothing. (AD-59, AD-45, AD-52, AD-6) *Rule to add:* "A step that moves a copy names what it releases. Only a listing settlement releases one reserved copy. A direct sale, an Archive purchase and every step of `ApplyWantedChoices` move free copies with nothing released. A test sells one free copy beside a deck block, a trade offer and an open listing, and checks that `reserved` is unchanged."

- **[high]** *Story A (`ApplyWantedChoices`' fallback)* reads "entrusts … an `order` copy left with no eligible order": when an order the table drew has since been cancelled, expired, withdrawn or served, the copy is left without its order and gets entrusted. *Story B (the recheck)* reads "rechecks that each `order` copy goes … to the order the table drew for it (`Failure(:best_order_changed)` otherwise)" and refuses the whole table. In Story A, a seller who chose to sell at the order's price, say 400, has the card minted at the entrustment value, say 50. The sale is turned into an entrustment the player never saw or confirmed, at an amount below the one they chose, and it uses up their daily cap. (AD-59, AD-42) *Rule to add:* "In `ApplyWantedChoices`, an `order` line whose table drew no order is entrusted. A drawn order that is no longer the best eligible order for its copy refuses the table with `:best_order_changed`, whatever happened to it. Nothing is entrusted that the table did not show as entrusted."

- **[high]** *Story A (the season's goal cleanup, AD-35)* removes, in the `SeasonPublished` handler's transaction, the game-set goals whose set the new season lacks. As a handler, it publishes no `GoalRemoved`. *Story B (the season's wanted refresh, AD-61)* enqueues the job that runs `RefreshWanted` "once per player with a goal or an open order". A player whose only goals were removed this way, and who has no order, matches neither condition. Their `goal` rows stay in `game_market_wanted` until some later event of theirs, possibly forever. Those rows keep sending them a `wanted` notice for every new listing of those games, and keep inflating `WantedCounts` for everyone. (AD-61, AD-35, AD-10) *Rule to add:* "The season's wanted job runs `RefreshWanted` for every player with a goal, an open order or a wanted row. Every path that removes a goal without publishing `GoalRemoved` (the completion and season handlers, the custom set cascade) is either listed with its refresh or covered by a test that compares `game_market_wanted` to a fresh `RefreshWanted` after each such path."

- **[high]** *Story A (direct sale entries)* reuses the settlement step's `sale_entries`, which computes the commission from, and stamps `settings_id` with, the version stored on the thing sold: for a listing that is the listing's version, so for an order it becomes the order's placement version. *Story B (the sale form, AD-42)* shows the commission of the current version, as AD-42 requires ("A direct sale's commission … read the current version"), and submits that version's token. When the commission changed after the order was placed, the seller confirms one amount and is charged another. The ledger entries also record a version that did not price them. The sum invariant holds, but money moves by a rule nobody confirmed. (AD-42, AD-59, AD-41) *Rule to add:* "Each ledger entry's `settings_id` is the version whose value priced it: for a direct sale, the `purchase` and `sale` carry the order's version (its price) and the `commission` carries the current version that `SellToOrder` checked against the seller's token, and the commission is computed from that version only."

- **[high]** *Story A (`CardListed` handler, AD-44)* writes `listed_above_order` on the (player, order) index. *Story B (`OrderServed` and `OrderExpired` handlers)* writes `order_served` and `order_expired` on the same (player, order) index. It follows AD-10's handler rule ("unique rows are inserted with `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`"), so the end-of-order notice is silently dropped whenever a listed-above line already exists. A third developer follows `Notice.record`'s upsert instead, which replaces the row. Separately, a cancelled or withdrawn order writes no notice and deletes none, so its `listed_above_order` line keeps pointing at an order that no longer exists. (AD-44, AD-10) *Rule to add:* "An order has at most one notice per player. An order's end (served, expired, cancelled, withdrawn) deletes its `listed_above_order` notice in that transaction, then writes its own end notice if it has one, by upsert replacing kind, listing and amount. `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` is for notices whose first write must win, which these are not."

- **[medium]** *Story A (the table's pre-check, AD-59)* refuses with `:invalid_choices` "stamps that `available` plus the validation's proceeds cannot pay", and counts each `entrust` copy's value among those proceeds. *Story B (the entrust step, AD-43)* skips copies over the daily cap, which is read from the day's `mint` entries. When the cap skips copies the pre-check counted, the netted post sends `available` below zero. AD-41's check then raises inside `Ledger.post`, and the player gets a 500 instead of a refusal that names each line. (AD-59, AD-43, AD-41) *Rule to add:* "The table's pre-check computes the entrustment proceeds from the cap left today (`CapLeft`) in the table's order, exactly as the entrust step will apply them, or leaves entrustment out of the proceeds. A post that would send a wallet below zero is a `Failure(:invalid_choices)`, never an exception."

- **[medium]** *Story A (`BestEligibleOrder`, AD-59)* reads "an open order" as `status = 'open'`. *Story B (the order screens, AD-58)* hides an order once `expires_at` has passed. `ExpireDueOrdersJob` runs every minute, so for up to a minute an order past its end can be served, which is the case "an order left open past its end" was meant to prevent. Meanwhile its owner is refused a new order on the same game with `:order_already_open`. A third developer "fixes" the gap by expiring orders when a page reads them, which makes a GET write. (AD-58, AD-59, AD-11) *Rule to add:* "An order is open while `status = 'open'` and `expires_at > now`. `BestEligibleOrder`, `WantedCounts`, the unique-open check of `PlaceOrder` (it expires a due order of its own player first, in its transaction) and `SellToOrder`'s recheck read `now` once and treat a due order as ended. Only `ExpireOrder` writes the status."

- **[medium]** *Story A (`ApplyWantedChoices`)* runs `SellToOrder`'s step once per line, and each run locks and refreshes that edition's price row (`refresh_edition_price`), so the rows are locked in the order of the table's lines. It also publishes one `CardListed` per listing, and that event's handler upserts `listed_above_order` notices of other players on (player, order) keys. *Story B*, a second seller validating a table of the same editions in the opposite order (or the nightly edition price refresh), takes the same rows the other way round. Neither shares a bank lock with the other, so they deadlock on edition price rows or notice rows. Neither row kind is ordered across lines. (AD-10, AD-47, AD-44, AD-59) *Rule to add:* "A composed operation collects its edition price refreshes and runs them after every move, in ascending card-season, and publishes events of one kind in ascending catalog card id. AD-10's lock order lists market notices after capacity rows."

- **[medium]** *Story A (the steps)* publishes each step's event where the step ends, as the code does today (`settle_sale` publishes tier events mid-step). *Story B (the handlers)* reads state as if its operation had finished. Inside `ApplyWantedChoices`, a `CardListed` published by an `auction` line before a later `order` line serves its order makes the notice handler write `listed_above_order` on an order served a moment later in the same transaction. It also runs `RefreshWanted` and the goal-completion handler before the remaining moves, so they act on half-applied holdings. (AD-59, AD-10, AD-44, AD-61) *Rule to add:* "In a composed operation, steps return their events, and the operation publishes them after all its writes, in a fixed order: allocations, then `OrderServed`, `CardsEntrusted`, `CardListed`, each kind in ascending card id. A handler never sees a half-applied operation."

- **[medium]** *Story A (`RefreshWanted`, AD-61)* is a plain `.call(player)` that deletes the player's rows and inserts new ones. *Story B (the season job, AD-61)* calls it straight from a job, which AD-3 forbids ("jobs never write a model directly"). Another developer wraps the call in an operation, which AD-10 then obliges to publish an event, an event AD-61 says is never published. A fourth story adds a new caller outside any bank lock. Two concurrent refreshes of one player then both delete and both insert, and the unique index on (player, card, reason) aborts the second operation, which is what AD-10 promises a duplicate never does. (AD-61, AD-3, AD-10) *Rule to add:* "`RefreshWanted` runs only while its player's bank lock is held: it asserts the lock and never takes one. Its inserts use `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`. The season job runs `Game::Market::RebuildWanted(player:)`, an operation that takes the bank lock, calls it and publishes nothing, listed with the housekeeping writes in AD-10."

- **[medium]** *Story A (removal, AD-46)* withdraws a banned player's orders, and `RefreshWanted` "writes none for a banned … player", so the withdrawal's refresh deletes their `goal` rows too. *Story B (unban, `Accounts::UnbanPlayer`)* publishes `PlayerUnbanned`, which no `Game` handler hears. The unbanned player's goals no longer count as wanted until their next event, and their wanted notices stop. (AD-61, AD-46) *Rule to add:* "`RefreshWanted` writes a banned player's rows like any other (every reader already skips removed players), and only deletion empties them." Otherwise `Game` must hear `PlayerUnbanned` and rebuild the player's rows under the bank lock.

- **[medium]** *Story A (`BuySlot`, AD-62)* adds the goal "through the goal step". *Story B (`AddGoal`, today)* answers `Success(existing)` with no event when the goal already exists, and fails with `:goal_set_complete` or `:goal_set_not_found`. One developer charges for the slot and returns the step's answer: the slot is bought, nothing is added, and the result is a success. Another rolls everything back. A goal added in another tab while the slot was being bought makes the first case real. (AD-62, AD-35) *Rule to add:* "`BuySlot` for a goal succeeds only when the goal step inserts a new goal. Any other outcome, an existing goal included, rolls the whole transaction back with that step's code, and no slot or PiCoin moves."

- **[medium]** *Story A (`BuyFromArchives`, AD-60)* refuses only a closed market, a stale version, an absent rarity, a lack of funds and a missing stock row, and "there is no daily purchase limit". *Story B (the shop button)* is a plain POST. A double click or a retried request buys two copies and destroys two prices, and the rate limit only caps how many. AD-8 closes the same race for trades ("a repeated send creates nothing"), and here nothing does. (AD-60, AD-40) *Rule to add:* "The shop form carries a single-use purchase token. `game_market_archive_withdrawals` is unique on player and token, so a repeated purchase finds its first withdrawal and answers it unchanged."

- **[medium]** *Story A (the table's recheck, AD-59)* compares each drawn order with the best eligible order computed under locks. *Story B (`PlaceOrder` by any third player)* runs under that player's bank lock only, and the table does not hold it. Any order placed at a higher price on any game of a large table, between the render and the click, refuses the whole table with `:best_order_changed`. The validation is rate limited, so a busy table can stay unsubmittable. (AD-59, AD-40) *Rule to add:* "`:best_order_changed` carries the lines it changed and their new best order. The table re-renders those lines with the player's other choices kept, and a refused validation does not count against its rate limit."

- **[low]** *Story A (`BuyFromArchives`)* locks the shared stock row before the allocation, the tier pass and its handlers (`GoalsReached`, `Achievements`, `RefreshWanted`) and holds it until commit. *Story B (other buyers of a popular Archive card)* queue behind that whole chain. This is the popular-row queue AD-43 and AD-9 set out to prevent. (AD-60, AD-43, AD-9) *Rule to add:* "`BuyFromArchives` decrements its stock row with one conditional `UPDATE … WHERE quantity > 0 RETURNING` as its last write before the ledger post; `:out_of_stock` answers no row returned; AD-10's lock order moves Archive stock rows after custom sets."

- **[low]** *Story A (the table's lines)* allows two lines of one card-season (some copies to orders, some to auction). *Story B (the pre-check)* checks free copies line by line. If 2 plus 2 copies pass against 3 free, AD-6's check raises in the middle of the moves. (AD-59, AD-6) *Rule to add:* "Lines are unique by card-season, or the pre-check sums each card-season's copies over every line against its free copies."

- **[low]** *Story A (`WantingPlayers`, AD-61)* returns every player with a wanted row on the game. *Story B (`WantedCounts`)* counts an `order` row only at its stored `rarities`. The notices handler then tells an order-only wanter about an edition their order cannot buy, while the count shown beside the card leaves them out. (AD-61, AD-44) *Rule to add:* "`WantingPlayers(card_season)` applies `WantedCounts`' rarity rule: a `goal` row matches every rarity, an `order` row only its `rarities`."

- **[low]** *Story A (`RefreshWanted`)* writes `rarities` by comparing price columns in SQL. *Story B (`Order.reaching`, AD-58)* is "the only formula". Two formulas, and nothing fixes the column type (code names or tier integers). (AD-61, AD-58) *Rule to add:* "`rarities` is a `smallint[]` of tier integers, written only from `Order.reaching`."

- **[low]** *Story A (the operations)* take `card_season_id:` (`SellToOrder`, `BuyFromArchives`). *Story B (the forms)* follow the signature and put the id in a hidden field, which AD-15 forbids. (AD-15, AD-59, AD-60) *Rule to add:* "Market volume forms name an edition by card slug and season number; the controller resolves the id."

- **[low]** *Story A (the bell, AD-44)* marks the grouped wanted line unseen "when a listing of it was created after the player last saw the line". *Story B* records "last saw" when the bell renders, so a GET writes. (AD-44, AD-11) *Rule to add:* "Seeing the grouped wanted line is recorded by the bell's existing read POST (`MarkBellRead`), a housekeeping write."

- **[low]** *Story A (`SumArchiveStocks`, AD-43)* copies the reconciliation's `REPEATABLE READ` to get "one read". *Story B (`BuyFromArchives`)* updates a stock row that the job then locks, and the job fails with a serialization error on every busy hour. (AD-43, AD-41) *Rule to add:* "`SumArchiveStocks` runs at `READ COMMITTED`: it locks rows, then computes deposits less withdrawals in one statement."

- **[low]** *Story A (`ExpireOrder`, AD-58)* acts with `require_account` and writes `order_expired` even for a deleted owner, whose notices `ReleasePlayer` already deleted. *Story B (`Notice.record`)* skips removed players today, but only in code. A rewrite that names its index (as AD-44's Costs ask) can drop that guard and leave a notice on a deleted account. (AD-46, AD-44) *Rule to add:* "No notice is written for a removed player; `Notice.record` refuses one, tested."

- **[low]** *Story A (`PackOpened`'s handler, AD-61)* runs the full `RefreshWanted` (`GoalNeeds` over every goal) on every opening. *Story B (AD-7)* keeps the opening's handlers inside the 200 ms p95. Neither looks at the other's budget. (AD-61, AD-7) *Rule to add:* "An event that only raises holdings deletes the player's `goal` rows of the credited games; only goal, custom set and order events rebuild the player's rows in full."

- **[low]** *Story A (`BuySlot`, in `Game`)* posts through `Game::Market::Ledger`. *Story B (AD-40)* maps `ActiveRecord::Deadlocked` to `:market_busy` only "in every market operation", so a deadlocked slot purchase is a 500. (AD-40, AD-62) *Rule to add:* "Every operation that calls `Ledger.post` answers `Deadlocked` with `:market_busy`."

- **[low]** *Story A (`ApplyWantedChoices`)* takes the bank lock of every drawn order owner for one transaction that runs every step and handler. *Story B (rate limits, AD-40)* limits listing per player, but a 50-line table lists 50 cards in one validation, and 30 owners' openings wait on it. (AD-59, AD-40, AD-7) *Rule to add:* "A table validates at most a fixed number of lines (a constant of the operation), and each line counts against the rate limit of its action."

## Counts

Critical 1, high 4, medium 9, low 11.
