# Abuse and privacy review: market volume

Reviewed 2026-11-20 against the uncommitted diff of EXPERIENCE.md (IA 58 to 62; Copy deck 205 to 215; Bell 292; Card detail and the market 334; Goal suggestion 339; Slot purchase 341; Market screen 405 to 410; Confier 422 to 425; Mettre en vente 426 to 427; MES ORDRES row 431; Order form 432 to 433; Shop screen 434 to 435; Delete dialog 559; Export 694; State Patterns 708 to 720; Flow 13, 1122 to 1130) and DESIGN.md (market components), `.memlog.md` 920 to 961, the three market-volume mocks, `spec-gameble/spec-gameble.md` (CAP-8, CAP-14, CAP-15, CAP-27, CAP-28, CAP-44 to CAP-49, Constraints), `spec-gameble/market-economy.md` and `forge-market-volume/forge-market-volume.md`. The two memlog overrides (wanted means missing from another player's goals only; no buy-now in the order form) are taken as the rule.

## Verdict

The flows handle the visible races well (an order served meanwhile, the last Archive copy, an order placed from another tab), and the seller never learns who placed an order. The weak points sit where the update adds new ways for money and cards to move, and none of the existing guards cover them. The Archives can be a laundering route and, while the Légendaire price is unset, a way around the 70,000 floor. Orders from banned or deleted accounts are not handled. Neither the alt-account signals nor the export cover the new flows. The wanted count and the best rival order are cheap to fake with alt accounts, and the narrower goals-only rule makes the count small enough to point at a specific person.

## Findings

- **[high]** Nothing stops the Archive price from going below a rarity's minimum price. While the Légendaire price is unset, that floor can be bypassed, and the shop can be swept to resell (`market-economy.md` The shop 71 to 76, Setting bounds 109 to 111; memlog 941; Shop screen 434, Archive purchase confirmation 435). At the simulated 5x, a Légendaire costs 5,000 in the shop, against a 70,000 minimum for a player's listing. The first player or script to see one buys it and relists it at 70,000 or more. CAP-48 says the price "caps the market without undercutting players", but neither the settings bounds nor the back-office row stop an administrator from saving a price that undercuts. The shop also has no per-account purchase limit, so a single account can empty an edition's stock as soon as it arrives. *Fix:* add a bound in `market-economy.md` Setting bounds, enforced in the back-office like the no-loss floor: "an Archive price is never below its rarity's minimum price, nor below 5 times its entrustment value". Keep the shop closed per rarity until that rarity's price is set: a rarity with no price has no tiles and no ACHETER. Add a per-account daily purchase limit per rarity, as a setting.

- **[high]** The Archives give alt accounts a way to launder cards and PiCoins that no signal watches (`market-economy.md` Alt-account signals 119; Shop screen 434; Confier wanted table result 425 "La carte rejoint les Archives"). An alt farms capsules (about 144 a day), entrusts the card the main wants, and mints PiCoins up to the 12,000 daily cap. The main then buys that exact edition from the shop. No trade, sale or order ever links the two accounts, so none of the six signals fire. Under the price gap above, this is also the cheapest way to get a Légendaire. *Fix:* keep, for each copy in the Archives, which account entrusted it (staff only, never shown in the shop), and add a signal to `market-economy.md`: "an account buys from the Archives a copy that an account tied to it entrusted within the last N days". Also list "Archive purchases and the entrusting account" among the facts a signal row shows (Back-office row, EXPERIENCE.md 436).

- **[high]** Nothing says what happens to an order, or to a player's goals, when its owner is banned or deleted (State Patterns, Participant removed 712; Delete dialog 559; Order served, expired or cancelled 720; Vos cartes voulues panel 409). Participant removed covers listings and bids only. A deleted account's open order still holds PiCoins that the Constraints say must be destroyed with its balance. If a seller serves it afterwards, a card goes to an account that no longer exists and money that should have been destroyed is paid out. A banned account's orders keep showing on sellers' banners, wanted tables and "Ordre <amount>" tiles, and a banned or idle account's goals keep counting in "<n> joueurs la veulent". The Delete dialog says "running listings withdrawn and bids released" and says nothing about orders. *Fix:* extend Participant removed to orders. On a ban or a deletion, every running order of the account is cancelled at once. On a ban, the money is released. On a deletion, the money is destroyed with the balance. The row moves to ANNULÉ. A banned or deleted account's goals stop counting as wanted. Add "open buy orders cancelled" to the Delete dialog's note and to the privacy notice.

- **[medium]** Alt accounts and idle accounts can inflate the wanted count cheaply. That floods Confier's second step for everyone (Wanted count 334; Confier 422 "one copy is set apart per player who wants the card"; VOULUE tag 408; MES SETS 342; Slot purchase 341). With the goals-only rule, a goal on a custom set of 50 games makes 50 games "wanted". One account with 10 goal slots can therefore flag up to 500 games, and ten alt accounts using only the free slots (5 goals of 50 games each) flag 250 games ten times over. Every honest player's Confier batch then stops at "Étape 2 sur 2". Sellers read "10 joueurs la veulent" and list at auction for demand that doesn't exist. Players who stopped playing months ago keep counting too. *Fix:* count a goal as wanted only when its owner signed in within the last 14 days, and never for an account that is banned or flagged by a signal. Count a custom-set goal only while the set has 15 games or fewer, or cap each player at, say, 30 wanted games across all their goals. That needs the author's approval, because it narrows CAP-44 further. Add "accounts whose goals overlap a tied account's listings" to the signals.

- **[medium]** Because the wanted count is small and goes up and down live, it reveals private goals and custom-set goals (Wanted count 334, read on load; Vos cartes voulues panel 409; `market-economy.md` Known limits 131; spec CAP-27 "Only its author sees a custom set or its name", CAP-8 private "no presence or activity"). The author accepted the count because it is an aggregate. The goals-only rule makes most counts 0 or 1, though, and a public profile already shows its owner's goals on game sets (CAP-28). A holder subtracts the public goals and can tell that the "1" left over comes from a private player or from a custom set. Then, by reloading the card detail, they see it go from 0 to 1 at the moment a friend says "je viens de me faire un set Supergiant". That shows the friend's private custom set and when they are active, which CAP-8 forbids for a private profile. *Fix:* compute the count once a day, at 00:00 UTC (the duel stats work this way), so a change can't be matched to a moment. Below 3, show "VOULUE" without a number ("Moins de 3 joueurs la veulent" in the card detail). If the author keeps exact live counts, the custom-set promise in CAP-27 and the profile visibility setting have to say that a custom set used as a goal is counted, without names, in the wanted counts.

- **[medium]** Showing the best rival order, with free cancellation, invites spoofing and price walking (Order form 432 "Meilleur ordre en cours"; memlog 945; `market-economy.md` Buy orders 59 to 69). The author decided to show it, and that decision stands: it breaks no other rule. A holder's alt can place a high order on a game nobody sells, though. Real buyers see "Meilleur ordre en cours : 400" and outbid it at 401 to be served first. The holder sells to 401, and the alt cancels for free. The alt never pays, because the holder serves the real buyer, who is first in line. The same fake order also changes the wanted table's prefill and the "Ordre <amount>" on other players' wanted tiles. The buyer mock's own note says this shows a competitor's price, which the spec gives only to the seller (CAP-47). *Fix:* count an order as "Meilleur ordre en cours" only once it has run for 1 hour, and never one placed by an account tied to the viewer. Add a signal: "orders cancelled within 24 h, repeatedly, on games that an account tied to them then sold". Optionally, keep a cancelled order's money held for 1 hour (cancellation stays free) so an order can't be placed and pulled at once.

- **[medium]** With no average price, the wanted table pre-selects any order, even one at the rarity floor. That steers sellers' cards into lowball orders (Confier wanted table 424 Prefill, "with no average price the order is preferred whenever one exists"; memlog 948, 959). With about 50 sales a week, most editions have no average price, so this is the usual case and not an edge case. A collector places orders at the Rare (78) or Épique (223) minimum on every game that other players have as goals. Every Confier batch that includes one of those cards then opens with À L'ORDRE selected, and "VALIDER" sells it. The forge rejected settling at floor price without a choice, because floor-price orders "capture supply below what bidders pay" (forge, Rejected). The author approved this assumption, but a pre-selected choice that one validation applies to the whole table comes close to that rejected design. *Fix:* when there is no average price, pre-select À L'ORDRE only if the order is at least 2 times the copy's rarity minimum. Otherwise pre-select ENCHÈRES at that minimum, and show "Aucun prix moyen" in the PRIX MOYEN cell so the seller knows the price is a guess.

- **[medium]** Sellers can repeat the "listed higher" and wanted bell lines to harass players, and those lines name the seller (Bell 292; Order bell lines 212 "<name> a mis <card> en vente à <amount>, au-dessus de votre ordre."; MES ORDRES row 431). Each listing of a game sends a named line to every order owner on it. A listing of a wanted card brings the grouped line back as unseen for every player who has it as a goal. A Common stamp costs 1 PiCoin, and a seller can cancel while no bid stands. So a list-and-cancel loop pushes the same name, which can be an offensive Steam persona, into the victim's bell over and over for a few PiCoins. Nothing says whether a second listing adds a line or replaces the first, or whether a cancelled listing removes it. *Fix:* keep one "listed higher" line per order, replaced by the latest listing and removed when that listing is cancelled. Don't put a name in the bell text ("<card> est en vente à <amount>, au-dessus de votre ordre."), since the Annonce shows the seller. Let a listing bring a grouped wanted line back only if the listing is still open when the line is computed, and at most once per card per day. Add "repeated listing and cancelling of the same game" to the signals.

- **[medium]** The alt-account signals don't cover the new flows' variants, and "high order" has no definition (`market-economy.md` Alt-account signals 119; spec CAP-37). The existing list does name direct sales to a high order from a tied account. It misses: (a) repeated sales at the rarity minimum between the same two accounts, made through orders (the existing signal is worded for sales, and order sales may not be included); (b) the main reading "Meilleur ordre en cours" and placing an order 1 PiCoin higher, which the tied account then serves, so the funnel always wins the queue; (c) a game's order filled by the copy with the lowest rarity: a Commune sold to a 5,000 order is a transfer of money, however high the order looks for the game; (d) the Archive and spoofing routes above; (e) PiCoins funnelled and spent on slots, which destroy them but finance a main. *Fix:* rewrite the signals to measure a direct sale against the rarity of the copy that served it ("an order sale at 10 times the copy's rarity minimum or more", the feed's threshold), state that every signal on sales includes direct sales from both entry points (the Mettre en vente banner and the wanted table's À L'ORDRE), and add the Archive, spoofing and list-and-cancel signals listed in this review.

- **[medium]** The export, the deletion and the feed don't mention any of the new data (Export 694; Delete dialog 559; spec Constraints "Market and personal data"; Market feed and lists 204). The Constraints list what the export includes: the PiCoin history, listings and bids, decks and duels. They say nothing about buy orders (placed, served, expired, cancelled, their prices), sales to orders, Archive purchases or bought slots. Nothing says whether an order sale or an Archive purchase of an Épique or Légendaire makes a feed entry naming the buyer ("<name> a acheté <card> pour <amount>.") and whether a private player's entry is hidden as it is for other feed entries. On deletion, the seller's name in a buyer's SERVI line ("Vendue par <name>") has to turn into "anonyme". *Fix:* add to the spec's Constraints, and to the privacy notice: "the export includes buy orders, sales to orders, Archive purchases and slot purchases". State in the Market feed row that a direct sale follows the feed rule for sales (Épique, Légendaire, or 10 times the minimum), that an Archive purchase never makes a feed entry, and that a private player's entries stay out of the feed. Add the SERVI line to the list of places where a deleted name reads "anonyme".

- **[medium]** Wash sales through orders can move the average price for less than an auction costs (`market-economy.md` Average price 55 to 57; Confier wanted table 424 "PRIX MOYEN"; Prefill 90 % rule). Nothing says whether direct sales count toward an edition's average price. If they do, two tied accounts can set the trimmed average of the last 5 sales with three order sales and no stamp. That average then drives the 90 % prefill, the PRIX MOYEN column, the Bourse and the feed threshold. *Fix:* decide it in `market-economy.md`. Either order sales don't count toward the average price (the safer choice, since an order is a price set by one buyer), or they count but sales between accounts flagged by a signal are dropped from it.

- **[medium]** The order form has no confirmation for an unusual price, while bids have one (Order form 432; `market-economy.md` Auction rules 49 "Confirmation of an unusual maximum"). An order is served instantly by the first holder who accepts, and by the lowest rarity it reaches, so a typo is lost at once ("1000" for "100": any Commune holder sells into it). Holders watching their banners profit from it, and the buyer can't take it back. *Fix:* reuse the Unusual maximum dialog when the price is at least 3 times the average price of the game's lowest-rarity edition, or, with no average price, at least 3 times the highest rarity minimum the price reaches. Its line names the rarity that will most likely serve the order ("Un exemplaire Commune peut servir cet ordre à ce prix.").

- **[low]** The seller could be shown their own order and sell to it (Best-order banner 427; Confier wanted table 424; Market screen 405, the empty search's "PASSER UN ORDRE" works on any game, owned games included). Nothing excludes the player's own order from "the best eligible order", while a seller can't bid on their own listing (CAP-31). A self-sale moves nothing but the commission, but it puts a meaningless sale into the counts and possibly the average price, and it confuses the result. *Fix:* "the best eligible order is never the player's own" in the banner, the table, Vos cartes voulues and the card detail's wanted line.

- **[low]** A direct sale gives one buyer the name of a private seller, which no listing ever published (MES ORDRES row 431 "Vendue par <name> · <rarity> S<n>"; Order bell lines 212; Names 421). An auction names its seller publicly, so the Names rule fits there. A direct sale has no listing, though: the line tells a stranger that this private player held this edition, and CAP-8 hides a private player's collection from friends. *Fix:* the SERVI line gives the edition and the date, "Servi le <date> · <rarity> S<n>", with no seller name. Staff keep the seller in the ledger.

- **[low]** Several parallel actions are only implied (Slot purchase 341; Archive purchase confirmation 435; Order on a game already 719). Two tabs at the goal limit can each open "UN 6E OBJECTIF ?" and buy two slots. A double press on ACHETER in the Archive dialog may buy two copies (only the order form says "a double activation submits once"). "One order per player per game" is described as a form behaviour. *Fix:* key a slot purchase to its number, so a second purchase of the 6th slot is refused and nothing is charged ("Cet emplacement est déjà acheté."). Give ACHETER and VENDRE MAINTENANT the rule that a double activation submits once. Write the order rule as holding "whatever the client does", as CAP-42 does.

- **[low]** Visitors and every player can read a dated log of entrusting in the shop (Market screen 405 "les plus récentes orders them by the day the copy joined the Archives"; Shop panel 410 "the copies entrusted last"; Boutique IA 59 open to visitors). Together with Le Grand Inventaire's live copy counts (its review, finding 4), anyone can tell when a scarce edition was entrusted and line it up with a player who said they were doing it. *Fix:* sort "les plus récentes" by week and not by day, and leave the entry date out of the tile and the dialog. Keep the visitor's shop rate-limited and cached, as the Grand Inventaire review asks for its pages.

## Checked, no finding

- **The seller never learns who placed an order** (Best-order banner 427), and the shop never names who entrusted a copy (Archive offer tile 407), so neither flow can be used to look up a buyer or an entruster.
- **The VOULUE tag reads only the viewer's own goals** and never shows to visitors (VOULUE tag 408); the wanted count never shows in the collection grid or to visitors (334, 409).
- **Orders don't make a card "wanted".** Wanted comes from goals only (memlog 939), so placing orders across many games can't flood the wanted counts.
- **A high order used to move money between alt accounts can be taken by any honest holder**, because the banner shows the best eligible order to every holder. Only orders on games nobody else holds escape that, and the reworded signal covers them.
- **The races drawn are handled:** an order served or withdrawn between drawing and pressing (717, results mock), the last Archive copy (716), an order placed from another tab (719, 433), the cap during the wanted table (718), a changed setting (Settings behind an open form).
- **Slots stay neutral spending** (spec Constraints): they don't change draws or duel strength, and the PiCoins spent are destroyed.
- **No buy-now in the order form** (memlog 940) removes the risk that an order silently buys at a stale price.

Not checked: the DESIGN.md token values and contrast figures, the copy's tone, and the mocks beyond the strings they draw for names, counts and orders.
