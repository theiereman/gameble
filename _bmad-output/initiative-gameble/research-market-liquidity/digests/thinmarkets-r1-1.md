## Claims

### a. Economics of thin markets

1. A working marketplace has to (1) be thick, drawing enough of the potential participants, (2) handle the congestion that thickness brings so participants can weigh enough options, and (3) be safe and simple enough that people prefer it to trading outside it. | https://www.nber.org/papers/w13530 | NBER (Roth, "What Have We Learned from Market Design?", later Economic Journal 118(527), 2008) | 2007-10 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high (read the abstract summary, not the full text) | academic
2. Roth's third criterion maps straight onto the decision context: a market fails when trading outside it is easier or safer. Free trades the app suggests are exactly such an outside option. | same as 1 | NBER | 2007-10 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (my application of the criterion, not Roth's) | mechanism
3. Roth's HBR article "The Art of Designing Markets" was published in October 2007. Its full text is paywalled, so its thickness/congestion/safety wording was not retrieved. | https://hbr.org/2007/10/the-art-of-designing-markets | Harvard Business Review | 2007-10 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high (date only) | academic
4. Barter needs a double coincidence of wants. In search models (Kiyotaki–Wright 1989/1991/1993), a medium of exchange arises because it cuts the search friction of random bilateral meetings. These models usually have multiple equilibria, so money is not guaranteed to be used. | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiyotaki%E2%80%93Wright_model_of_money ; https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/a-contribution-to-the-pure-theory-of-money/ | Wikipedia; Princeton (abstract) | undated | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (snippets of abstracts or summaries) | academic
5. Inference (not from a source): the friction that money removes is finding a counterpart who has what you want and wants what you have. A system that suggests trades does that matchmaking directly, which takes away the main advantage a currency market has over barter. | n/a | n/a | n/a | accessed 2026-11-19 | low (inference to test) | mechanism
6. Andrew Chen's "atomic network" is the smallest network that delivers value on its own. The "hard side" of a marketplace often needs subsidies early on to guarantee initial liquidity. | https://www.antoinebuteau.com/the-cold-start-problem-book-summary/ ; https://www.indiehackers.com/post/how-to-kickstart-a-marketplace-lessons-learned-from-andrew-chens-book-the-cold-start-problem-b0fdf81f6b | secondary summaries of Chen, The Cold Start Problem (2021) | undated | accessed 2026-11-19 | low (snippet, secondary; primary not read) | mechanism
7. Lehdonvirta and Castronova, *Virtual Economies: Design and Analysis* (MIT Press, 2014), covers markets, money, faucets and sinks. A secondary summary says mild inflation encourages spending and market liquidity, while deflation leads players to hoard currency. | https://www.antoinebuteau.com/virtual-economies-book-summary/ ; https://mitpressbookstore.mit.edu/book/9780262535069 | reader summary; MIT Press | 2014 (book) | accessed 2026-11-19 | low (secondary snippet; book text not read) | academic

### b. How games bootstrap or consolidate liquidity

8. On August 16, 2022 (patch 9.2.7), World of Warcraft merged commodity listings across all realms in a region: "Auction Houses across all realms on a given region will now share commodity listings (gems, herbs, flasks, consumables, etc.)". Non-commodity items (gear) stayed per realm. | https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23833174 | Blizzard (official patch notes) | 2022-08 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high (read) | dev-statement
9. Blizzard's stated goal, reported in the press: the AH "should look and feel exactly the same; it'll just have way more buyers and sellers available to you for commodities". The press framed the change as one that "benefit[s] smaller servers". | https://www.pcgamer.com/world-of-warcraft-is-changing-how-the-auction-house-works-to-benefit-smaller-servers ; https://eu.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/region-wide-auction-house-927-ptr/366451 | PC Gamer; Blizzard forums | 2022-07 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (snippet) | dev-statement
10. Design pattern in WoW: consolidate the market only for fungible, interchangeable goods (commodities) and keep unique items local. | same as 8 | Blizzard | 2022-08 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (inferred from the scope of the change) | mechanism
11. Albion Online's "Black Market" is an NPC that only places buy orders for equipment. Its prices respond to supply and demand, and what it buys goes back into the game as loot. It gives sellers a buyer of last resort. With no buy order, the item cannot be sold there. | https://www.albiondatabase.com/guides/black-market ; https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2017/06/albion-onlines-new-black-market-feature-is-sounding-really-interesting | community guide; GamingOnLinux (pre-launch dev info) | 2017-06 / undated | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (snippets; no official doc read) | mechanism

Proposals from a small, early MMO community (Pantheon): a searchable listing database with prices, where the trade itself still happens by direct whisper; local bazaars with NPC brokers; a tax on automated trade to favour manual trade; and regional markets to "help control monopolies". | https://seforums.pantheonmmo.com/content/forums/topic/8405/classic-trade-vs-ah-compromise | Pantheon official forums | undated | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (read; player proposals, not shipped) | player-sentiment
13. In FFXI, players proposed a cross-server AH with a roughly 10% tax on purchases from low-population servers. Players argued Square Enix would not do it because server transfers are revenue and dev resources are scarce. | https://www.ffxiah.com/forum/topic/54195/cross-server-auction-house | FFXIAH | 2019-11 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium (read) | player-sentiment

### c. Player voice: empty or dead markets (3 communities read: WoW, FFXI, Pantheon; 1 more from a snippet: Fallout 76 on Steam)

14. WoW, low-population realms: "there really isn't anything for sale". The cause is "lack of supply, and lack of demand, both due to a low population". A few experienced traders dominate, and everyone else buys from them or does not trade. The author calls a cross-realm AH a "savior". | https://www.engadget.com/2013-02-15-do-we-need-a-cross-realm-auction-house.html | Engadget/WoW Insider (columnist and player) | 2013-02 | accessed 2026-11-19 | high (read) | player-sentiment
