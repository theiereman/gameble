### c. Sinks and trade-ups that create demand for duplicates

15. CS2's October 2025 change letting five Covert skins trade up into a knife/glove reprised the market within hours: Covert input skins rose up to ~10x (some reports >2,000%), knives/gloves fell 30-60%; estimates of total market value lost range from ~$1bn to $1.8bn. A sink that consumes items turns them into market demand rather than pulling volume away from the market. | https://refrag.gg/blog/cs2-skins-update-trade-ups ; https://spilled.gg/valves-counter-strike-2-update-crashes-knife-prices-wipes-estimated-1bn-skin-market | Refrag; Spilled (snippets; commercial/market-tracker estimates) | 2025-10 | accessed 2026-11-19 | medium | quantitative

## Patterns (inference, not evidence)

- Every studio that refused trading cited one of two reasons: abuse (bots, multi-accounts, scams, real-money trading) or keeping the game about collecting and playing (Hearthstone, PTCGP). Marvel Snap adds a supply argument: if copies are cheap to make, a market has nothing to price.
- The one game that kept trading after a backlash (PTCGP) fixed it with demand visibility (wishlists) and a cheaper cost, not with a market.
- Where a market and private trading coexist (Neopets, Steam), the auction format was the least used: Neopets players negotiated on the Trading Post and used the Auction House only to close deals; Steam duplicates move through 1:1 matching more than the market (low confidence).
- Sinks feed markets when items are scarce and priced (CS2), but in a closed economy a sink paid in its own currency (Hearthstone dust, MTG wildcards, PTCGP shinedust) replaces the market entirely.
