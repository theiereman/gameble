- (note) Today the counter already lets B change both sides; the spec's CAP-11 success line 'sent only when each side holds a card' is what the idea breaks.
- (assumption) Value is both: the social signal (help yourself) and not having to guess what the friend wants; the epic 18 'missing from theirs' filter covers part of the guessing.
- (crack) An offer with nothing in it can read as begging ('give me your Legendary') to the recipient.
- (assumption) Multi-account farming through gifts is mitigated by sign-in through Steam only: an account costs creating a Steam account, not one click (author).
- (lock) No gift: an open request cannot be accepted as it stands; the recipient picks at least one card from the proposer's collection and sends it back, or declines. A swap always has two sides (author).
- (lock) Only the offer side may be empty: the proposer says what they want, the friend says what they want in return. Never an empty request, never both sides empty (author).
- (decision) With no gift, the friend's answer is an ordinary counter-proposal: its offer reserved, the proposer's cards checked at acceptance, 7 days restarted, the proposer keeps the veto. No new reservation rule, no new state.
- (lock) On the friend's board, in place of ACCEPTER, a short text says to choose the cards wanted in return; the server still refuses accepting a trade whose offer is empty (a stale tab or a hand-made POST). No stored mode: the rule follows from the empty side (author).
- (lock) No separate gesture for the proposer: sending with an empty offer is the open request. A mistaken empty send costs little: nothing is reserved, the friend cannot accept, the proposer can cancel (author).
- (crack) A forgotten offer is sent as an open request without warning; accepted because the mistake costs nothing and is cancellable.
- (lock) One rule at every send, proposal or counter: the sender may leave their own side empty, never the other's. It lets the proposer say 'not that one, pick something else' without leaving the trade (author).
- (direction) A note attached to a proposal (author's idea), e.g. to frame an open request; raises free-text moderation questions.
- (decision) The note shows as information on the trade, read by both players (author).
- (direction) Author prefers a discussion window on trades over a note: free text between friends, on the trade.
- (crack) A trade chat is a feature of its own: player-written text needs reports, hiding and back-office reading (CAP-15), export and erasure with the account (CAP-14), bans, private profiles, unread badges, live delivery and rate limits.
- (lock) The open request ships without a note; a discussion window on trades is a separate idea, to be forged on its own (author).
- (kill) A note on the proposal: dropped from this idea in favour of a separate trade discussion idea.
